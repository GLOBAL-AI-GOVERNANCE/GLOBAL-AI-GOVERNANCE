# NYC Validation Case Study (Proposed Legislation)

**Status: Proposed. Not law. Source-locked 2026-10-14.**

Primary source: Gary Marcus prepared remarks, NYC City Council Committee of the Whole hearing, 5 October 2026. One-page testimony, not a full transcript.

## Source claims (not established facts by this record)

- General-purpose agents with broad internet access have led to tens of thousands of hacking incidents.
- Chatbots implicated in suicides and mass murder.
- Current federal administration unwilling to address risks due to ties to large technology companies.
- Recent events suggest OpenAI may have fired employees who were whistleblowing.

Marcus explicitly rejects imminent human extinction as the most credible framing while arguing for serious present harms.

## Proposed controls (T2026-2602 and companions)

- Independent third-party validation before market access / deployment in NYC.
- Defined assessment domains (task performance including accuracy, precision/recall, calibration, distribution shift; determinism; latency/throughput; data provenance; disparate impact; data handling; safety).
- Human-operable shut-down capability.
- Evaluator interest disclosure.
- Deployment-readiness certification.
- Cyber Command rulemaking for qualifications and details.
- $25,000 per-instance penalties (business and validator).
- Companion: whistleblower protection clarification (T2026-2604) and civilian enforcement incentive (T2026-2605).

Bills introduced on agenda 8 October 2026. Status as of source lock: Introduction. Not enacted.
